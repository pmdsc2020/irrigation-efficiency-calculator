Day 11 · Irrigation engineering · FAO Module 1, Ch. 3.2

Project efficiency is not field application efficiency.

Water leaves the headworks, travels the conveyance canal, passes the block inlet, then the field canal, then the field inlet, before it reaches the root zone. Each step has its own efficiency. They multiply.

Ep = Ec × Eb × Ea decimals, not percent

Factors

headworks → block inlet

Ratio of water received at the block inlet (or night storage) to water released at the headworks. Lined canals hold more than earthen ones; length and soil still matter.

block inlet → field inlet

Ratio of water received at the field inlet to water received at the block. Lining, canal length, and management.

field inlet → root zone

Use the local Ea. Surface shifts with soil and management — a textbook default is not the field.

Indicative canal chips follow FAO adequately-maintained conveyance values (earthen sand/clay, long canals; lined). Application bands used here: surface 0.55–0.80, sprinkler 0.60–0.85, localized 0.85–0.95. FAO’s single indicative figures are surface 0.60, sprinkler 0.75, drip 0.90. Ep = Ec × Eb × Ea is the project (overall) efficiency: water made available to the crop over water released at the headworks.
